About

I am a student of law and life. I am agentic, and I believe that cogent action can create effective change. As a law student, two of my public interventions, at 19 and at 21, led to the decriminalisation of a mobile phone game and to the first live-streaming of court proceedings in the history of the Indian judiciary.

A trained lawyer (bar call 2023, India), I served as Judicial Clerk to the Chief Justice at the Supreme Court of India, contributing to judgments, case management strategies, national legal policy and speechwriting. I read for the Master of Law at the University of Cambridge, graduating First Class and winning an academic prize. At Cambridge, I researched constitutional change, asking how to operationalise a governing principle once declared. I secured a First on the dissertation as well as on the Law, Technology and Society paper. I use socio-technical inquiry, constitutional law and legal-policy frameworks to contribute to AI governance.

I like to solve complex problems, convey complex ideas in written and verbal form, and ideate outside the box. I have debated (and won) at the Cambridge Union, was featured on BBC, and have hosted Lady Hale at my college.

Projects

CiteMeRight

Hack the Law Cambridge 2026 · Finalist · Anthropic Prize winner

At Hack the Law Cambridge 2026, our team built CiteMeRight, a contextual citator that goes beyond the binary question of whether a case has been overruled. Law lives in grey areas, so CiteMeRight is built around reliability: it surfaces verifiable context and shows an auditable chain of reasoning for every conclusion. We reached the final round and won the Anthropic Prize.
